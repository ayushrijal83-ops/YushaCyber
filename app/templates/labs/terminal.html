State-changing operations should not normally be performed through GET — a link, image tag, or prefetch can trigger a GET with no user intent at all. This training site has no GET route for /transfer at all.

{# ── CSRF vs. XSS ── #}
CSRF vs. XSS
XSS

Attacker-controlled script executes in the victim's own application context — it can read the page, not just send one request.

CSRF

The attacker causes the victim's browser to send an authenticated request — it cannot read the response, only trigger the action.

XSS ≠ CSRF, but XSS can sometimes undermine CSRF defenses (e.g. by reading a page-embedded token) — not explored further in this mission.
